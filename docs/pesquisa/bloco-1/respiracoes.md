# Detecting and removing breaths and long pauses in spoken-word video, locally (TypeScript/Node + ffmpeg, optionally a small ONNX model), for an Electron editor with a pt-BR UI

## Summary
Professional tools fall into two groups. Breath processors keep the timing and lower the breath: iZotope RX Breath Control has a Gain mode and a Target mode, and it was rebuilt with neural networks in RX 12; Waves DeBreath works the same way; Auphonic 'Remove Breathings' mutes breaths without cutting them and warns that full (100 dB) removal sounds unnatural. Pause removers cut and close the gap: Descript 'Shorten word gaps' (target gap, e.g. 200 ms), Premiere's transcript Pause filter (Extract/Lift, deletes the whole pause), Resolve 20.2 'Ripple Delete Silence' (Pre Head/Post Tail/crossfade), Audition 'Delete Silence', and auto-editor/TimeBolt/FireCut/CapCut. None of the pause removers recognises breaths as such; they only use level thresholds. That is why breaths often survive: they are 16–29 dB below the speech but well above the noise floor. Phonetics papers give usable starting values. Breath noises in speech mostly last 200–600 ms (means about 290–540 ms; inhalations before an utterance are longer). Their spectral centre of gravity is below 2 kHz, while aspiration and /s/ are higher, shorter and louder. They are aperiodic, and they are usually separated from the words by 23–96 ms of silence. Between 47% and 83% of pauses contain one. For voice activity detection (VAD), Silero VAD v6.2.3 (Sept 2026) is the best permissive choice: MIT licence, 2.3 MB ONNX, 512-sample windows at 16 kHz, about 0.2 ms per window on one CPU thread. WebRTC VAD is clearly weaker, and TEN VAD's licence has a non-compete clause. The only directly reusable open ML breath detector found is Respiro-en (MIT, Interspeech 2024, IoU 0.836 / P 0.924 / R 0.897 on English audiobooks). It needs ONNX export and may not transfer well to pt-BR phone footage. PodcastFillers (which has breath labels) and CrisperWhisper are non-commercial. ASR word timestamps are only reliable to about 100–200 ms, and Whisper's tokenisation folds pauses into word timings. Gaps between words are therefore good candidates, but every cut boundary must be refined acoustically. Recommendation: Silero VAD plus a transparent TypeScript DSP classifier that runs inside pauses (optionally limited to ASR word gaps), with markers on the timeline that the user reviews. Default treatment: lower breaths by 20 dB with 25 ms ramps, never below the measured noise floor. Optional: shorten pauses with a ripple cut to a 120–300 ms gap, with an asymmetric guard (at least 100–150 ms kept after a word, 50–100 ms before the next), 10 ms fades at the joins, and an optional alternating 110–115% punch-in to hide the jump cuts. Brazilian Portuguese needs special care: in one São Paulo study about 22% of word-final unstressed vowels were devoiced and 8% deleted. Breath-like noise attached to the end of a word must be treated as part of that word. This codebase needs four things first: split and ripple operations, preview audio routed through Web Audio gain nodes (the CORS setup already allows it), an export that uses one input per media file, and filter scripts to stay under the 32,767-character Windows command-line limit. Every number here is a starting value from tools and papers and has to be checked on a small hand-labelled set of the creator's own pt-BR footage. The web-fetch budget ran out late in the research, so Székely et al. 2019 and arXiv 2509.15473 were not read, and the Waves and CapCut details come from secondary sources.

## Choice
Detection: option B (Silero VAD v6 ONNX + a transparent TypeScript DSP breath classifier inside pauses, limited to ASR word gaps when a transcript exists), with Respiro-en exported to ONNX as an optional phase-2 verifier. Treatment: by default, lower breaths in place (-20 dB, 25 ms ramps, never below the noise floor). Optionally, shorten long pauses with a ripple cut to a 120–300 ms gap (asymmetric guards, 10 ms fades, frame-snapped), with an optional alternating punch-in zoom. Everything goes through a review layer of markers on the same timeline and is applied as one draft or proposal (the same draft flow used for AI edits).

## Why
1) The professional breath tools (iZotope, Waves, Auphonic) lower breaths rather than delete them, and Auphonic explicitly warns that full removal sounds unnatural. Lowering also keeps captions, text and lip sync intact and fits the current data model with the least engine work. 2) Creators also want pacing, which every creator tool gets by cutting pauses to a minimum gap (Descript example 200 ms; TimeBolt keeps 150 ms after speech 'to protect trailing consonants'). Breaths sit inside those pauses, so cutting pauses while keeping silent parts removes most breaths with far fewer jump cuts than cutting every breath. 3) Silero is permissive (MIT), tiny (2.3 MB) and about 100x real time on one CPU thread, and it beats WebRTC VAD; TEN VAD's licence is unacceptable. But no VAD separates breath from silence, and the only open breath model (Respiro-en) was trained on English audiobooks. A DSP classifier based on well-documented breath acoustics (200–600 ms, 16–29 dB below speech, centre of gravity below 2 kHz, aperiodic, silent edges) is explainable, tunable, has no download, and gives confidence scores for review. 4) ASR word timestamps are useful to find gaps but are only accurate to about 100–200 ms, so cut edges are snapped to acoustic evidence. 5) Brazilian Portuguese devoiced final vowels and word-final /s/ make breath-like noise right after words common, so a silent-dip requirement and post-word guards are essential.

## Concrete plan
0) Prerequisite core work (also needed for feature 1, 'cortar áudio'): add ops splitClip{clipId, at} and removeRange{start, end, ripple: true}. removeRange shifts later text and caption clips, shortens clips that span the range and shifts their keyframes. Add per-clip gain regions. Route preview audio through Web Audio. In export, use filter scripts and one input per media file. Detection and lowering can ship before cutting.
1) Decode (Electron utilityProcess or worker, not the renderer): spawn ffmpeg-static (^5.3.0, which is FFmpeg 6.1.1) with 'ffmpeg -nostdin -hide_banner -loglevel error -i <path> -map 0:a:0 -vn -ac 1 -ar 16000 -f f32le pipe:1' and read a Float32Array. Compute min/max waveform peaks per 10 ms in the same pass for the timeline. Cache under userData/analysis/<hash(path, size, mtime)>-v<ANALYSIS_VERSION>.
2) VAD: bundle silero_vad.onnx (v6.2.x, MIT, 2.3 MB, pin its SHA-256) in resources/models (electron-builder asarUnpack for **/*.node, **/*.dll and models). Runtime: onnxruntime-node 1.31.x (MIT; exclude non-win32 binaries via 'files' globs). If the captions feature adopts sherpa-onnx-node 2.0.x (Apache-2.0), use that runtime instead; never load two onnxruntime.dll copies in one process. Loop: x = concat(context[64], pcm[i..i+512]) as a [1,576] float32 tensor, state [2,1,128] float32 (zeros at start), sr = int64 16000 (scalar). Outputs are positional [prob, newState] (check session.outputNames). Next context = last 64 samples. Upsample probabilities to the 10 ms grid. Hysteresis: speech at p >= 0.5, non-speech below 0.35 (Silero's own neg_threshold is threshold - 0.15).
3) Features (pure TypeScript in src/core/audioAnalysis.ts, unit-testable with vitest), per 10 ms hop with a 25 ms Hann window and 512-point FFT: rmsDb, zcr, centroidHz, flatness (300–7000 Hz), hfRatio = E(4–8 kHz)/E(0.1–8 kHz), lfRatio = E(80–300 Hz)/E, periodicity = max normalised autocorrelation over 2.5–12.5 ms lags, crest factor. Rolling ±5 s references: noiseDb = 10th percentile of rmsDb where p < 0.2; speechDb = median of rmsDb where p > 0.7. Local references cope with phone AGC.
4) Pauses: maximal non-speech runs of at least 150 ms. If transcript words exist, use word gaps of at least 150 ms intersected with acoustic non-speech, and never cut inside word spans shrunk by 30 ms on each side. Refine edges: speechEnd = last frame after the word end with p > 0.5, periodicity > 0.5 or rmsDb > speechDb - 20; onset found the same way.
5) Breath events inside a pause: candidate frames need rmsDb - noiseDb >= 6, speechDb - rmsDb >= 10, periodicity < 0.45, centroid between 400 and 3200 Hz, and hfRatio < 0.35. Merge gaps of 40 ms or less; keep events of 120–1000 ms. Score from 0 to 1 = 0.25*dur + 0.25*level + 0.2*centroid + 0.15*aperiodic + 0.15*edges, where: dur = 1 for 200–700 ms, falling linearly to 0 at 100 and 1200 ms; level = 1 when 14–32 dB below speech, 0 at 8 dB or less; centroid = 1 at 2 kHz or below, 0 at 3.5 kHz or above; edges = 1 if a dip (at most noise + 4 dB for at least 20 ms) separates the event from the previous speechEnd, otherwise 0.3. Labels: score >= 0.7 'respiração' (pre-checked); 0.4–0.7 'revisar' (unchecked); below 0.4 hidden. Transients under 60 ms are 'estalo/clique'. An event touching speechEnd with no dip is 'incerto' (pt-BR devoiced vowels, /s/ tails). Flag 'música/ruído' when the noise floor is high and stationary or tonal (optional CED tagging); in that case default to no cut and warn.
6) Treatments (shared pure functions in src/core/audioEdit.ts):
(a) Lower (default for breaths): region from max(ev.start - 0.01, speechEnd + 0.03) to min(ev.end + 0.01, onset - 0.02); depth = max(preset (Leve -12, Natural -20, Forte -30), noiseDb + 2 - eventPeakDb), i.e. never below the floor and never muted; 25 ms ramps.
(b) Cut (pauses; breath pauses only in the 'Acelerado' preset): cut a pause [s, e] when e - s >= minPause (Natural 0.6 s, Dinâmico 0.4 s, Acelerado 0.25 s) and keep G (0.3 / 0.2 / 0.12 s). Split G as keepAfter = max(0.08, 0.6*G) after speechEnd and keepBefore = max(0.04, G - keepAfter) before the onset, moving the kept window onto silent frames. Snap both cut edges to the 1/fps grid and to the lowest-energy frame within ±10 ms. Apply 10 ms fade-out and fade-in at each join. Lower any breath left inside the kept part using (a). Skip a cut that would leave a speech or video segment shorter than 0.4–0.5 s; merge adjacent cuts.
(c) Room tone (phase 3): loop the quietest 0.5–1 s of non-speech, joined with 50 ms equal-power crossfades, only under regions lowered below the floor or lifted.
Zoom on cuts (optional): alternate scale 1.00 / 1.12 per resulting segment with hold keyframes, anchored at the canvas centre (or at the face or track box once the tracking feature exists). Leave segments under 1 s unchanged.
7) Data model. Analysis cache (outside the project JSON): Float32Arrays plus events. Project JSON: VideoClip.gain?: {id, s, e, db, fadeMs, source: 'breath'|'manual'}[] in source seconds; project.audioMarks?: {id, mediaId, kind: 'breath'|'pause'|'click', s, e, conf, state: 'pending'|'accepted'|'rejected'}[] in source seconds, so marks survive trims and rejections are remembered when detection is re-run. New Op variants: splitClip, removeRange, setGainRegions{clipId, regions}, and a macro cleanSpeech{clipId|'all', breaths: 'lower'|'cut'|'off', breathDb: -20, pauses: {minMs: 400, targetMs: 200}|null, minConfidence: 0.7, zoomOnCuts: false}, expanded into primitive ops when the proposal is prepared (like the existing 'animate' macro).
8) Preview (src/renderer/Preview.tsx): chain MediaElementAudioSourceNode(el), then GainNode(master*clip.volume), then GainNode(envelope), then the destination; set el.volume = 1. Schedule linearRampToValueAtTime about 100 ms ahead from el.currentTime using the shared gainAt(). The media:// protocol already sends access-control-allow-origin: * and elements set crossOrigin='anonymous', so Web Audio will not be silenced by CORS. Pre-roll the next segment's element (seek + muted play) at least 300 ms before a join and reuse a small pool per media file instead of one element per segment.
9) Export (src/core/export.ts): per clip, add 'asetnsamples=n=256,volume=eval=frame:volume='1-0.9*clip((t-a)/f,0,1)*clip((b-t)/f,0,1)-…'' after aresample and before adelay. Here t is clip-relative (subtract c.in), and FFmpeg 6.1 supports clip/between/if/lerp in expressions. Add afade=t=in:d=0.01 and afade=t=out:st=<dur-0.01>:d=0.01 on segments created by cuts. Today every clip adds '-ss -t -i path' to the command line, so with N segments, write the graph with '-filter_complex_script <tmp>' (supported in 6.1) and open each media file once (asplit + atrim), to stay under Windows' 32,767-character command-line limit and avoid N decoders. Better parity long term: render the mixed audio in TypeScript (decode 48 kHz f32 once per media, apply the same envelope and fades) to a temporary WAV used as the audio input.
10) MCP: add tool analyze_audio{clipId}, which runs or caches the analysis and returns a summary of at most 1.5 KB (counts, total seconds, estimated time saved per preset, music/noise flags). Add list_audio_marks{clipId, kind, minConf, offset, limit<=40}. propose_edit accepts cleanSpeech, so the AI never lists hundreds of ranges and stays within the 6 KB economy budget.
11) UI (pt-BR, same screen): an Inspector section 'Respirações e pausas' for the selected video clip with a [Detectar] button and progress bar. A marker lane under the clip waveform (blue = respiração, grey = pausa, hatched = revisar). Summary line, e.g. '42 respirações · 18 pausas longas · o vídeo fica 0:31 mais curto'. Selectors 'Respirações: Abaixar volume (recomendado) | Cortar | Manter', 'Força: Leve/Natural/Forte', 'Ritmo: Natural/Dinâmico/Acelerado', and a toggle 'Zoom nos cortes'. Buttons 'Aplicar selecionadas', 'Revisar uma a uma', 'Descartar'. Review mode plays from 1.5 s before to 1 s after each item, has an A/B switch ('Antes/Depois'), and keys Enter = aceitar, Backspace = rejeitar, ←/→ = navegar, Espaço = ouvir de novo, plus auto-advance. Bulk actions: 'Aceitar todas acima de X%' and 'Rejeitar incertas'. Everything is one draft and one undo step.
12) Evaluation before enabling pre-checked defaults: hand-label 20–30 min of representative pt-BR creator footage (Audacity label tracks). Measure event precision and recall with ±100 ms tolerance, and IoU. Targets: precision >= 0.9 for pre-checked items, recall >= 0.8, and zero clipped words in a 50-cut listening test. Add vitest fixtures with synthetic harmonic 'vowels', 4–8 kHz '/s/' noise, 400–2500 Hz breath-like noise with 300 ms envelopes, and silence.
13) Phase 2: export Respiro-en to ONNX (opset 17, dynamic time axis, 30 s chunks with 1 s overlap), port the features exactly, keep threshold 0.064 and the 20-frame minimum, and auto-check only items where it agrees with the heuristic. Ship it as an optional download after benchmarking on the labelled set.

## Risks
- Thresholds come from German/English lab speech and English audiobooks, and may not transfer to pt-BR phone or lapel-mic footage; validate on a labelled pt-BR set first
- pt-BR devoiced or deleted final vowels (22.4% / 8.1% in a São Paulo sample) and word-final /s/ ~ [ʃ] look like breaths and can be clipped; the silent-dip rule, post-word guards and ASR spans are needed
- Breath pauses occur roughly 9–17 times per minute, so cutting all of them produces dense jump cuts; the preview (one HTMLVideoElement per clip, long-GOP phone video) may stutter at each join without pre-roll and pooling
- Export with many segments: the current command line adds one '-ss/-t/-i' per clip, which hits the 32,767-character Windows limit and spawns N decoders; switch to filter scripts and one input per media file
- Music beds, other voices and noisy rooms: cuts chop music and lowering ducks it; detection gets worse. Detect, warn, and default to lowering only
- Phone AGC and noise suppression change the noise floor over time; use rolling local references
- ONNX runtime packaging: the npm package is about 307 MB unpacked across platforms (prune it); two onnxruntime.dll versions in one process can collide on Windows; native module loading must be tested on clean Windows 10/11 machines
- Respiro-en: English audiobook domain, checkpoint stored via Git LFS (self-host the ONNX), CPU speed not benchmarked
- Intellectual property and licences: Dolby US10825464 (active, estimated expiry 2036) covers breath-frame classification with a mitigation gain combined with a levelling gain; get legal review before combining auto-levelling with breath suppression. Avoid TEN VAD (non-compete clause), CrisperWhisper and the PodcastFillers annotations (non-commercial)
- Some tool details (Waves via a blog, CapCut via third-party articles, Premiere via community posts) were not checked against vendor manuals

## Fallback
If no ONNX runtime can be shipped or loaded: use DSP-only detection (option A, with energy plus periodicity as the VAD and relative thresholds), and ffmpeg silencedetect at noise floor + 6 dB for pauses, with lower confidence (nothing pre-checked). If the split/ripple and many-segment preview/export work is not ready: ship detection, review markers and in-place lowering first, and show the long pauses as markers the user can trim by hand; enable the cut mode later.

## User-facing constraints
- Everything runs offline on the PC's CPU; no audio or video is uploaded and no GPU is needed.
- The first analysis of a video takes a few seconds per 10 minutes of footage (estimate: about 5–15 s on a typical laptop, to be measured) and shows a progress bar; results are cached, so reopening is instant.
- The installer grows by about 10–25 MB (ONNX runtime plus a 2.3 MB voice-detection model) unless the captions feature already ships an ONNX runtime. A later optional 'IA de respiração' model would be a one-time download of about 15–35 MB.
- Detection is not perfect: some soft breaths will be missed and some word endings, 's' sounds or whispered final vowels may be flagged, so reviewing before export is recommended. 'Abaixar volume' is the safe default.
- Cutting breaths or pauses shortens the video and makes the image jump at each cut (jump cuts). 'Zoom nos cortes' partly hides this. Texts and captions after a cut move earlier automatically.
- If the recording has background music or other voices in the same audio, cutting will also cut the music and lowering will also lower it; the app warns in that case.
- Noisy rooms and phone noise suppression reduce accuracy; best results come from a microphone close to the mouth in a quiet room.
- Audio changes sound the same in the preview and in the exported MP4.

## Product questions
- When you say 'remover respirações', do you want breaths cut out (shorter video, jump cuts) or made silent/quieter while keeping the timing? Which should be the default?
- Should shortening long pauses be part of the same one-click action ('Limpar fala'), and which default pace: Natural (keep about 0.3 s), Dinâmico (about 0.2 s) or Acelerado (about 0.12 s, also cuts breath-only pauses)?
- Should 'Zoom nos cortes' (alternating about 110–115% punch-in) be on by default when cutting? Should it centre on the face once tracking exists?
- Do your typical recordings contain background music, sound effects or a second person speaking in the same audio track?
- Is it OK to pre-select high-confidence detections automatically, or should everything start unselected for one-by-one review?
- Can you provide 5–10 minutes of typical raw footage (kept local) so the thresholds can be tuned and verified for your voice, microphone and room?
- May the AI assistant include breath and pause cleanup automatically when asked for a 'more dynamic' edit (always as a draft), or only when explicitly asked?
- Do you also want filler words ('é…', 'ahn', 'hum', 'tipo', 'né') detected? This requires the speech-recognition transcript.

### Outros problemas que a checagem encontrou

- Clean-Windows blocker (most important): if onnxruntime-node is the runtime, require() fails on a PC without the VC++ 2015-2022 x64 runtime (at least 14.44, the linker version of the 1.31.1 binaries; MSVCP140_ATOMIC_WAIT.dll and MSVCP140_1.dll exist only in recent redists). Electron 45.3.4 does not ship these DLLs. Node loads addons with LOAD_WITH_ALTERED_SEARCH_PATH, so the executable's folder is not searched: put msvcp140*.dll and vcruntime140*.dll next to onnxruntime.dll in app.asar.unpacked, or run the latest vc_redist from NSIS. Microsoft's STL 17.10 note warns that an older msvcp140 loaded first can cause null dereferences in mutex code (e.g. https://riverbankcomputing.com/pipermail/pyqt/2025-November/046378.html crash in MSVCP140!mtx_do_lock). The rec's 'test on clean Windows' step should explicitly cover a VM with no VC++ runtime installed.
- Installer size: onnxruntime-node's Windows onnxruntime.dll is 31.7 MB, about 8.2 MB after xz -9. Left unpruned, DirectML.dll (18.5 MB, 8.8 MB xz) and dxcompiler.dll (18.0 MB, 5.7 MB xz) add about 36.5 MB installed (about 14.5 MB compressed). DirectML, d3d12 and dxgi are delay-load imports and dxcompiler is not imported at all, so pruning them for CPU-only use looks safe but must be tested. The real delta is about 8–23 MB compressed and 32–69 MB installed.
- Runtime choice is inconsistent across blocks. The STT recommendation ships sherpa-onnx-node 2.0.1 (static CRT, bundled ORT 1.28.2), but sherpa's Vad exposes no probabilities and no generic session, so the probability-based design and phase-2 Respiro-en cannot run on it. Options: (a) keep onnxruntime-node in its own utilityProcess, adding the VC++ DLLs and avoiding the onnxruntime.dll clash with sherpa; (b) use onnxruntime-web (WASM) in a worker, with no native or VC++ dependency; (c) port Silero to TypeScript. For (c), 6.2.x ships silero_vad_16k.safetensors (1.24 MB, about 310k parameters) and a 40-line tinygrad reference model, but my quick numpy port agreed on only 98.5% of speech decisions against ONNX on the repo's sample (mean |Δp| 0.016), so parity work would be needed.
- Use the batched model for offline analysis. silero-vad 6.2.2+ ships silero_vad_16k_sequence.onnx (inputs input[n,576], h and c [1,1,128]; outputs speech_probs, hn, cn). It gave bit-identical probabilities (max |Δ| = 0) to the per-window loop and ran 10 min of audio in 0.83 s versus 2.32 s in onnxruntime-node 1.31.1 (Linux VM, 1 thread). It also removes about 18,750 async session.run calls per 10 min.
- Preview/export parity risk: scheduling GainNode ramps relative to el.currentTime cannot guarantee alignment within the plan's 25–30 ms ramps and guards. The media-element clock and AudioContext.currentTime are separate, and output latency (outputLatency/getOutputTimestamp in the spec) is tens of ms on Windows. Preview may duck word tails or let breath onsets through, while export (sample-accurate filters) does not, which contradicts 'sounds the same in preview and export'. Compensate with getOutputTimestamp/outputLatency and re-anchor after seeks, or play a pre-rendered processed AudioBuffer for the clip under review.
- Snapping rules in step 6(b) conflict. The 1/fps grid (33 ms at 30 fps) and 'lowest-energy frame within ±10 ms' generally cannot both hold, and grid rounding of ±16.7 ms can eat most of the 40 ms keepBefore guard in 'Acelerado'. Decide which wins (e.g. round only the timeline position and keep the audio source in/out sample-accurate) and re-check the guards after snapping. Phone footage is often VFR, so a nominal 1/fps source grid may not match real frame times.
- Export at scale: on local ffmpeg 7.0.2, 200 ripple segments from one 10-min file via asplit+atrim+adelay+amix took 13.2 s (about 50 MB RSS), against 4.7 s (about 42 MB) with asplit+atrim+concat; both produced the correct length. The plan works, but use concat for the sequential segments of one clip and amix only across overlapping sources. On the Windows 6.1.1 binary only -filter_complex_script exists; '-/filter_complex' is 7.0+.
- Linux dev/CI (the repo's e2e runs on Linux): npm install of onnxruntime-node on linux/x64 downloads CUDA 12 EP binaries from NuGet by default (install-metadata.js lists linux/x64: ['cuda12']). install-utils throws if the download fails, which can break npm ci. Set ONNXRUNTIME_NODE_INSTALL=skip.
- Measurements made on this Linux machine with ffmpeg-static use FFmpeg 7.0.2, not the 6.1.1 that ships for Windows. Re-run the filter and envelope tests with the Windows 6.1.1 binary before relying on them.
